What VLT means

VLT stands for visible light transmission: the percentage of light the film lets through. A lower number is darker. 70% is barely noticeable, 35% is a common legal limit for front side windows, and 5% or 3% is often called limo tint.

Check your state's tint law first

Every state sets its own limits, and they are usually different for the windshield, the front side windows and the rear windows. Many states only allow a light strip at the top of the windshield and require front side windows at or above a set VLT, often in the 25% to 50% range. Look up your state's current rules before you pick a shade, because a ticket or a failed inspection costs more than the film. Keep in mind that film stacks with factory glass tint, so the final VLT ends up darker than the number on the roll.

DIY or a pro?

Flat side windows are the easiest place to start. Work in a clean, dust-free garage, spray the glass and film with slip solution, squeegee from the center out, and trim with a sharp blade. Curved rear windows usually need a heat gun to shrink the film, which takes practice. If you want a perfect result on a newer car, a professional install may be worth the cost.

Frequently asked questions

Is 5% tint legal?

On front side windows it is illegal in most states. Some states allow very dark tint on rear windows. Check your state's current law.

Will tint block my phone or GPS?

This film is non-metallic, which the maker says avoids signal interference. Metallic films are the ones that can cause problems.

Can I tint the windshield?

Most states only allow a strip along the top of the windshield. Full windshield tint is usually illegal or tightly limited.

How long does DIY tint take?

Plan a few hours for side windows on a first attempt. Leave windows rolled up for a few days afterward so the film can cure.
